Study Notes — Geometry Essentials (15 minutes)

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, you can:

1. Select and apply the correct area or volume formula (rectangles, triangles, trapezoids, circles, prisms, cylinders, spheres, cones) and compute the result within 60 seconds per item, including shaded-region subtraction problems.
2. Determine missing side lengths and angle measures in right triangles using the Pythagorean theorem, the 45-45-90 and 30-60-90 ratios, and similarity scale factors, and state which property justifies each step.
3. Use SOHCAHTOA (sin = opposite/hypotenuse, cos = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan = opposite/adjacent) to compute any side of a right triangle from one acute angle and one side, and verify each answer in the Desmos testing calculator within one keystroke sequence.

Key Rules and Formulas

  • Triangle: angles sum to 180°; area A = ½bh (height must be perpendicular to the base); third side x satisfies |a − b| < x < a + b.
  • Pythagorean theorem: a² + b² = c²; memorized triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 7-24-25, 8-15-17 (and their multiples).
  • Special right triangles: 45-45-90 → sides x, x, x√2; 30-60-90 → sides x, x√3, 2x (short leg opposite 30°).
  • Similar triangles: corresponding angles equal, corresponding sides proportional; a shared scale factor k multiplies every length.
  • Circle: C = 2πr = πd; A = πr²; arc length = (θ/360)·C; sector area = (θ/360)·πr² = ½ · arc · r.
  • Volume: prism/cylinder V = Bh (cylinder: πr²h); sphere (4/3)πr³; cone (1/3)πr²h; pyramid (1/3)Bh. Recasting a solid preserves volume.
  • SOHCAHTOA: sin θ = opp/hyp, cos θ = adj/hyp, tan θ = opp/adj — the reference angle decides which side is “opposite.”

Academic Vocabulary

Area — the measure of a surface region, expressed in square units. Example: The area of the circle is 36π square centimeters.
Volume — the capacity of a three-dimensional solid, expressed in cubic units. Example: Recasting the sphere into a cylinder preserves its volume but not its shape.
Sector — the region bounded by two radii and the arc between them. Example: A 45° sector occupies one-eighth of the circle’s area.
Hypotenuse — the longest side of a right triangle, opposite the right angle. Example: The ladder is the hypotenuse of the right triangle formed by the wall and the ground.
Scale factor — the constant ratio linking corresponding sides of similar figures. Example: Because the triangles are similar, the tree’s height equals the person’s height multiplied by the scale factor.
Pythagorean triple — three positive integers satisfying a² + b² = c², such as 7-24-25. Example: Recognizing the 7-24-25 Pythagorean triple removes the need to compute a square root.

Worked Examples with Thinking Steps

Example 1 — Shaded region (circle and inscribed square). A square is inscribed in a circle of radius 6. Find the area inside the circle but outside the square.
Step 1: Compute the whole: circle area = π(6)² = 36π.
Step 2: Relate the figures: the square’s diagonal equals the circle’s diameter, 12.
Step 3: Square area = diagonal²/2 = 144/2 = 72 (equivalently side = 12/√2 = 6√2, area = 72).
Step 4: Subtract: 36π − 72 ≈ 41.1 square units. Desmos: type 36 × pi − 72 then enter to check the decimal.
Example 2 — 30-60-90 triangle. The hypotenuse of a 30-60-90 triangle is 20. Find the longer leg.
Step 1: Recall the ratio x : x√3 : 2x, where 2x is the hypotenuse.
Step 2: Set 2x = 20, so x = 10 (the short leg).
Step 3: Longer leg = 10√3 ≈ 17.3. Desmos: type 10√3 or 10 × sqrt(3) then enter. Trap warning: never multiply the hypotenuse by √3 — the hypotenuse is the largest value.
Example 3 — SOHCAHTOA. In a right triangle, an acute angle measures 27° and the hypotenuse is 15. Find the side opposite the angle.
Step 1: Label relative to the 27° angle: opposite = unknown, hypotenuse = 15.
Step 2: Opposite and hypotenuse → sine: sin 27° = opp/15.
Step 3: opp = 15 · sin 27° ≈ 6.8. Desmos keystrokes: 15 × sin(27°) then enter → 6.809…; confirm Deg mode (the SAT Desmos calculator defaults to degrees — do not toggle to radians).

Common Traps and How to Avoid Them

Trap 1 — Diameter used as radius. If the item says “diameter 10,” then r = 5 and area = 25π, not 100π (a 4× error). Avoid: underline r or d in the prompt before choosing a formula.
Trap 2 — Non-perpendicular height. The slanted side of a parallelogram is not its height; the height forms a right angle with the base. Avoid: draw the perpendicular dashed line first.
Trap 3 — Missing fractions. Omitting ½ (triangle), ⅓ (cone/pyramid), or 4/3 (sphere) is the most frequent volume error. Avoid: copy the formula from the SAT reference sheet before substituting.
Trap 4 — Form mismatch. If choices contain both 25π and 78.5, decide — from the question stem — whether the answer is required “in terms of π” or “to the nearest tenth.” Avoid: re-read the final sentence of the stem.
Trap 5 — Sector vs. arc. A sector has area (square units); an arc has length (linear units). Wrong choices frequently present the arc length for a sector-area question. Avoid: check the units requested in the stem.

Quick Mnemonic and Pre-Answer Checklist — P.A.T.H.

P — Perpendicular? Is the height perpendicular to the base?
A — Answer form? Units, “in terms of π” or decimal, rounding as directed.
T — Trap-check. Radius or diameter? Whole or shaded? Sector or arc?
H — Half/Third. Apply ½ (triangle), ⅓ (cone), 4/3 (sphere), and θ/360 (circle fractions) before finalizing.
